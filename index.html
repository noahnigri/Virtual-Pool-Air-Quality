SECTION 04

The research base

Every threshold, coefficient, and vulnerability multiplier in the calculator maps directly to peer-reviewed publications. Summaries here are paraphrased; source links go to the original papers.

CRITICAL REVIEW · 2020

Inorganic chloramines — occupational exposure limit review

Emmett et al. · Critical Reviews in Toxicology

Consolidates the toxicological and epidemiological evidence on inorganic chloramines and proposes an occupational exposure limit. This is the paper the tool's tier thresholds are calibrated against.

WHO reference value: 0.5 mg/m³ · Parrat irritation onset: 0.36 mg/m³ · ANSES proposed OEL: 0.3 mg/m³
READ PAPER →
DOSE-RESPONSE · CHILDREN

Lung hyperpermeability and childhood asthma from pool attendance

Bernard et al. · Occupational and Environmental Medicine

Studied 1,881 schoolchildren and found a dose-response relationship between cumulative chlorinated-pool attendance and asthma prevalence. Younger start ages showed stronger effect — the basis of the calculator's ×2.5 multiplier for children under 12.

Pool attendance was the single most consistent predictor of lung epithelium permeability
READ PAPER →
CONSENSUS REVIEW

Childhood asthma and environmental exposures at swimming pools

Weisel et al. · Environmental Health Perspectives

Multi-author consensus review commissioned by NIEHS. Establishes that cumulative exposure (bather-hours) drives inflammation and bronchial hyper-responsiveness in swimmers and lifeguards.

Occupational levels typically measured 0.42 ± 0.24 mg/m³ — right in the irritation-onset band
READ PAPER →
CFD STUDY · 2022

Outdoor air & ACH effects on trichloramine — CFD simulation

Proulx & Hallé · Frontiers in Built Environment

Numerical study of a Montréal natatorium. Found that ASHRAE minimum ventilation (3.6 ACH, 23% OA) produced 1,910 ppb at the swimmer's face — 2× the room average. Basis for the tool's 2.0× stratification factor.

Increasing to 100% OA reduced weighted-average NCl₃ by 37%
READ PAPER →
OEL PROPOSAL · SWISS

Assessment of occupational trichloramine exposure — Swiss OEL

Parrat et al. · Ann. Occup. Hyg.

Measured symptoms among 128 lifeguards and swim instructors across Swiss pools. Recommended an OEL of 0.3 mg/m³ with irritation onset at 0.36 mg/m³ (95% CI 0.30–0.52).

This paper's 0.36 mg/m³ is the "Tier 2 CAUTION" boundary in the calculator
READ PAPER →
DOSE-RESPONSE · ITALY · 2013

Airborne NCl₃ levels & health symptoms in pool workers

Fantuzzi et al. · J. Expo. Sci. Environ. Epidemiol.

Twenty Italian indoor pools, 128 employees. Measured a mean airborne NCl₃ of 0.65 ± 0.20 mg/m³ (range 0.20–1.02). Directly demonstrated dose-response for red eyes, runny nose, voice loss, and itchy eyes above 0.5 mg/m³.

Above 0.5 mg/m³: OR 2.9 for runny nose, 3.2 for red eyes, 3.6 for voice loss — validates WHO threshold
READ PAPER →
ADOLESCENTS · CUMULATIVE · 2009

Chlorinated pool attendance & adolescent respiratory health

Bernard, Nickmilder, Voisin & Sardella · Pediatrics 124(4):1110

Cohort of 847 adolescents aged 13–18 across chlorinated vs. copper-silver pools. Cumulative exposure predicted asthma prevalence in a dose-dependent way. Adolescents with >1,000 pool-hours showed dramatically elevated risk.

Teens with >1,000 cumulative hours: 8× the asthma risk vs. non-chlorinated-pool reference · population-attributable risk 63.4%
READ PAPER →
RECENT · CANADA · 2024

Personal vs. stationary trichloramine exposure in pool workers

Ahmadpour et al. · Ann. Work Expo. Health 68(6):593

Six Canadian indoor pools, 60 workers. Compared fixed-station environmental sampling to actual personal breathing-zone measurements. Found stationary measurements over-predict worker exposure by ~50% because workers move between pool and lower-exposure zones (offices).

Stationary GM 0.57 mg/m³ vs personal GM 0.30 mg/m³ — the tool reports both room-average and swimmer-zone for this reason
READ PAPER →
STANDARDS

WHO recreational-water & CDC MAHC

World Health Organization · U.S. CDC

Two authoritative bodies of guidance the calculator is designed to be compliant with. WHO sets the 0.5 mg/m³ NCl₃ reference; CDC's Model Aquatic Health Code sets the cyanuric-acid ceiling and water-chemistry practices.

CDC MAHC: indoor pools should keep CYA < 15 ppm — the calculator flags violations

SECTION 05

What actually works

Four intervention categories, ranked by the calculator's own physics for effect on the score. Each links back to the input that controls it.

Source-capture ventilation

Deck-level exhaust that catches the dense chloramine layer at the water surface before it reaches the breathing zone. Backed by Proulx & Hallé (2022): correct return placement matters more than raw airflow volume.

→ ADJUST OUTDOOR-AIR FRACTION

Purpose-built dehumidification

Dedicated pool units (Dectron, Desert Aire, PoolPak, Seresco) instead of repurposed comfort HVAC. Handles latent load without dropping supply air onto the water surface, which drives evaporation.

→ ADJUST SUPPLY ACH

Secondary oxidation (UV-C / ozone)

Medium-pressure UV at 40–60 mJ/cm² destroys chloramines in water before they can off-gas. Cassan et al. 2006 measured 65–78% reduction. The single highest-leverage water-side intervention available.

→ OPEN FULL SIMULATOR TO ADJUST UV-C

Water chemistry discipline

Combined chlorine is the primary chloramine driver via Henry's law. Free-Cl residual, pH, and CYA (indoor pools shouldn't have any) all shift the equilibrium. Managed through daily test-kit measurements — no capital cost.

→ ADJUST COMBINED CHLORINE

The IAQ Mission is dedicated to raising awareness about the importance of indoor air quality in aquatic facilities, and the impact it has on swimmer health, performance, and long-term participation in the sport.
